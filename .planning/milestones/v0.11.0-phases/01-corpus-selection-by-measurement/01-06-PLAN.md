<objective>
Run the spike. Fetch and index candidates incrementally, record what each actually produces,
compute and freeze the per-kind threshold by a deterministic rule, and lock the corpus set on
that evidence.

Purpose: this is the phase's reason for existing. The trap it exists to catch is already in this
repository's history — v1.0 Phase 1 established that codegraph-go's own idiomatic Go source
legitimately produces **zero** `overrides` and **zero** `type_of` edges, so a corpus set can
under-cover the nine-kind vocabulary while every test stays green. That is a silently narrowed
oracle. This plan replaces reputation with recorded measurement.

Output: `corpora/observations.json` (generated evidence), `corpora/selection.json` (curated
policy), `docs/CORPUS-MEASUREMENT.md` (generated), and `corpora/manifest.json` with the locked
flags flipped.

Ordering is deliberate: `overrides` and `type_of` are measured FIRST, because they are the two
kinds most likely to come up short and the ones whose failure would force the widest search.

## Synthetic coverage does not satisfy FIXT-01 — the criterion wins over the decision

An earlier draft of this plan let a kind be marked "covered synthetically" and treated that as
satisfying the coverage claim. **That is wrong and is reversed here.**

ROADMAP Phase 1 success criteria 2 and 3 require a non-zero measured count **attributed to a
named repository**, and criterion 3 names `overrides` and `type_of` specifically. D-16 permits a
*recorded* behavioral-corpus fallback. Those are different statements, and the earlier draft
quietly used the second to weaken the first. **A decision cannot amend a success criterion by
implication** — this repository amends criteria deliberately and on the record.

Two further facts made the earlier draft worse than merely permissive. The fallback was not
implemented or measured anywhere in this phase (the task file lists carry only the record files
and the generated document — no behavioral-corpus source), so a kind could have been marked
synthetically covered with **no indexed corpus and no observed count behind it at all**. And the
plan's own automated verification already required a named repository with a positive count, so
its executable gate and its prose acceptance disagreed.

**The resolution adopted here: narrow the plan to what the criterion demands.**

- Phase 1 requires third-party coverage for all nine kinds.
- If the bounded search (D-17) exhausts with a kind still uncovered, **that is a BLOCKING PHASE
  FAILURE.** Halt, record the state, and escalate for a roadmap/requirements decision. Do not
  lock a set, do not mark the kind synthetic, do not report the phase complete.
- `corpora/selection.json` retains a `syntheticKinds` field so a future, explicitly-amended
  requirement has somewhere to record one — but Plan 01-07's guard treats a non-empty
  `syntheticKinds` as a FAILURE, so it cannot silently satisfy the claim in this phase.

**Why narrowing rather than building the fallback.** Making the fallback real in-phase means
authoring behavioral-corpus source, indexing it and pinning its identity — that is FIXT-05's
territory in Phase 2, and Phase 1 is a *blocking* spike whose boundary explicitly excludes
corpus authoring. Widening the blocking phase to build a fallback that the criterion does not
accept anyway would be the worst of both.

**Why the risk is low in practice.** `synthesizeOverrides` needs an embeds/extends/implements
edge plus a same-name-and-arity method — guava's forwarding-class hierarchy is literally that
shape. `emitTypeOfRef` needs an explicitly typed variable declaration — Java and C# field and
local declarations are literally that shape. The bounded search has capacity for six candidates
beyond the shortlist across four languages. Escalation is the honest handling of a case we do
not expect to hit, not a likely outcome.
</objective>
