<assumption_delta_decision>
**Signal:** the deterministic assumption-delta detector flagged a `pluralization` signal on
"…while carrying `.zip` archives alongside the raw binaries `codegraph upgrade` consumes." The
release asset shape generalizes from one form (raw binary) to two (raw + `.zip`).

**Primary noun:** the raw per-platform binary asset, `codegraph_<tag>_<goos>_<goarch>` — UNCHANGED
and still primary.

**Decision: `add-alongside` — accepted, deliberately NOT `promote`.**

The checkpoint's default guidance is that a generalization should usually promote the new general
representation to primary and demote the specific one. This phase does not do that, and that is
correct: `codegraph upgrade`'s wire contract resolves the raw asset name at runtime through
`internal/upgrade.releaseAssetName()` and downloads that exact byte stream with no extraction
step. Promoting `.zip` to primary would require every already-shipped binary in the field to
learn to extract an archive — a change to a contract held by binaries we cannot update. D-02/
Finding 1 is therefore *preserved byte-unchanged*, not amended: the raw entry keeps
`formats: [binary]` and its exact `name_template`, and the zip is added as a second `id:`-keyed
entry.

**What would force a later promote:** any future change that makes an archive form the asset
`codegraph upgrade` itself consumes — for instance a decision to ship notarized, stapled
`.pkg`/`.dmg` payloads as the canonical download, or an upgrade path rewritten to extract. Until
then, add-alongside is the correct model, not debt to pay down.

**Invariant that encodes this:** `TestRawArchiveEntryStaysBinaryFormat` — the raw entry must stay
`formats: [binary]`, demonstrated RED by mutation. This is REL-09's own success criterion and it
is authored in Task 1 below.
</assumption_delta_decision>
