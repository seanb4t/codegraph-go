<known_limitations>
## What this plan's gates prove, and what they do not

Addresses review concern (MEDIUM): the previous draft's `must_haves` said the audit gate
"distinguishes 'scanned and clean' from 'the scan itself failed'" while a sibling truth described
a three-way classification — but only the clean path and the scan-error path are ever exercised.
The wording is narrowed here rather than the claim being quietly kept.

| Branch | Implemented | Demonstrated in this phase | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| CLEAN — parseable output, zero advisories | yes | yes — the ordinary green run | This is the steady state. |
| SCAN ERROR — unparseable/empty/unexpected output | yes | yes — Task 2 runs the target with the registry unreachable and captures the distinct message and non-zero exit | This is the branch D-15 exists for. |
| ADVISORIES PRESENT — parseable output, one or more advisories | yes | **no** | Exercising it needs a vulnerable input. D-15 declined a `vuln:selftest`-shaped pinned-advisory fixture because a pinned JS advisory rots as packages are yanked or patched, and RESEARCH searched for a low-maintenance pin and found none. The lockfile-count sibling proves the scan had real input; it deliberately does not prove the detector fires. |

The honest claim this phase makes is therefore: **the gate proves the scan ran against real
input, and it never lets a failed scan read as clean.** It does not claim to have proven the
detector can fire. `T-02-07-07` carries the accepted-risk record and the revisit condition.
</known_limitations>
