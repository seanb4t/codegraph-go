not violate is only observable under genuine concurrent multi-process use, and running

    Instrument each tab to record a client-side receipt timestamp per event. Then run three
    scenarios and record evidence for each:

    FAN-OUT AND TIMING. Trigger at least three REAL re-indexes, spaced deliberately, waiting each
    time until every tab has received that generation before triggering the next. Every tab must
    receive every generation, and the recorded inter-arrival gap per tab must be at least the
    trigger spacing. A test asserting only that N messages arrived is vacuous here — silent
    buffering delivers all N at the end and passes. The blocking structure is the assertion.

    BACKPRESSURE. Put one tab into a state where it cannot process events promptly (occupy its
    main thread for longer than the trigger interval, so the browser stops draining), keep the
    other two normal, and trigger several re-indexes. Record: the two normal tabs received every
    generation with unchanged timing, and the slow tab, on recovering, is at the NEWEST
    generation. The coalescing decision means the slow tab legitimately skips intermediate
    generations — record the skipped count as evidence the coalescing happened, not as a failure.

    RECONNECT. Stop and restart the server process while the tabs are open. Record each tab's
    reconnect attempt timestamps. Assert the gaps GROW across consecutive attempts and that the
    three tabs' attempt times are not identical — jitter is what prevents a synchronised wave.
    Assert every tab is receiving events again afterwards, and that its resume cursor advanced
    rather than restarting from zero.

    Record the per-tab attempt-count and total-attempt figures explicitly: a reconnect storm is a
    high attempt count in a short window, so the record must carry the number, not a verdict.
  </action>
