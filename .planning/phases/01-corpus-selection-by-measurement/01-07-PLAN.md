<objective>
Turn the measurement record into a guard that keeps holding, and wire the corpora into CI with
a per-corpus cache whose miss path falls through to a real fetch.

Purpose: D-07 makes the record re-runnable rather than frozen once, and D-08 splits the cost by
trigger — a cheap check on every run against the committed record, and a re-index-and-diff leg
only when the pin manifest changes. FIXT-02 criterion 5 requires a CI run to restore the
corpora from cache with a miss falling through to a fetch rather than to a skip.

Output: the coverage guard, three Taskfile targets, one new step in the existing CI test job,
and a new path-filtered sibling workflow modelled on `linux-cross-canary.yml`.

The guard is written so that bumping a pinned SHA in a way that kills `type_of` coverage fails
loudly instead of passing silently. That is the whole point of D-07, and it is the reason the
positive-count assertions below are not optional decoration.

## Cache mechanism — a corrected premise, and the decision it forces

**CONTEXT.md D-13, RESEARCH.md and PATTERNS.md all state that this phase introduces the
repository's first CI caching. That is false, and the plan corrects it here rather than
inheriting it.** All three reached that conclusion by grepping for the literal vendor string
`actions/cache`, which genuinely has zero hits. Enumerating every `uses:` line across all
workflows instead shows the repository already caches in **nine** places:

`namespacelabs/nscloud-cache-action@c5f8dab7560444c4bf8dbc64f1b203431873c547 # v1.6.1`, with
`cache: go`, at `.github/workflows/ci.yml` lines 60, 203, 232, 356, 386, 415 and
`.github/workflows/bench.yml` lines 112, 374, 473 — always paired with `actions/setup-go`
carrying `cache: false`, so the Go module cache is delegated to Namespace rather than to
setup-go. This is a **locked maintainer decision** (v1.0 Phase 10 D-06, chosen at full scope),
not an open field.

**Decision: use the established mechanism — option (a).** `namespacelabs/nscloud-cache-action`,
not `actions/cache`.

**Why it fits, verified against its actual interface rather than assumed.** The Namespace action
takes `cache`, `detect`, `path`, `fail-on-cache-miss`, `github-token` and the two spacectl
inputs, and emits a `cache-hit` output. **It has no `key` input at all** — it mounts a
persistent NVMe volume on the Namespace runner and caches *paths*, with no upload or download
phase. At first reading that looks like it cannot express D-13's "one entry per corpus keyed
`repo@sha`". It can, and by a better route: **the corpus destination path already embeds the
pinned SHA.** Plan 01-04's `Entry.Dir` resolves to `<root>/<slug>@<sha>`, so each corpus
occupies its own content-addressed subdirectory inside the cached root. A pin bump produces a
*new path*, which is absent from the volume by construction and therefore must be fetched.
D-13's actual requirement — a hit is provably the right tree, and a prefix match on a SHA can
never hand back a different commit's tree — is satisfied structurally, and there is no
prefix-matching feature to disable because there are no keys.

**D-13's stated premises do not survive the correction, and are not carried forward as fact.**
D-13 justified per-corpus granularity by "GitHub gives a repo 10 GB of total cache with LRU
eviction" and by a single manifest-hash-keyed blob being "likelier to hit the ceiling alone".
Both describe **GitHub's** cache service. This workflow runs on
`namespace-profile-linux-amd64-4x8` — the same runner class as `ci.yml`'s `test` job (line 48)
— where the cache is a provisioned volume, not a 10 GB LRU pool. That ceiling reasoning is
therefore inapplicable and is recorded here as retired rather than repeated. What survives from
D-13 is its real intent: content-addressing, and a miss that fetches rather than skips. Both
hold, by the path scheme above and by the unconditional fetch in Task 3.

**What this avoids.** Adding `actions/cache` would have meant a second, differently-shaped
caching mechanism sitting beside nine instances of an established one, on runners where its
documented behaviour is an open question — and would have silently overridden a locked
maintainer decision. Choosing the established mechanism also means this phase adds **no new
supply-chain surface at all**: the action and its pinned commit are already trusted in nine
places in this repository.
</objective>

## STRIDE Threat Register

| Threat ID | Category | Component | Severity | Disposition | Mitigation Plan |
|-----------|----------|-----------|----------|-------------|-----------------|
| T-01-07-01 | Tampering | mutable cache volume poisoning | high | mitigate | **This repository has already reasoned about exactly this risk twice, in committed comments, and acted on it both times** — `release.yml:113-120` deliberately withholds the cache action from the release job because that job holds `id-token: write` and "a release build should not read from a mutable cache any PR on any branch can populate" (review synthesis finding S2; T-01-29), and both canaries omit it to keep their failure surface minimal. RESEARCH.md filed cache poisoning as "out of immediate scope for THIS phase's design"; that is the wrong call for this repository and is corrected here. **Which side of the line this job falls on:** the `corpora` job requests `contents: read` only, holds no `id-token: write`, signs nothing and publishes nothing, so it sits entirely outside the signing boundary `release.yml`'s exclusion protects. It is the same trust posture as `ci.yml`'s `test` job, which already reads from the same volume in six places. **The independent control that makes a mutable volume acceptable here:** the assertion step re-verifies every restored tree's checked-out HEAD against the manifest's pinned SHA before anything is indexed, so a poisoned or stale directory fails the job rather than being measured — a guarantee neither caching mechanism provides on its own. The drift leg is a second, independent control: a substituted tree would produce different measured counts and fail the diff. This repository's CI triggers on pull request and push to the default branch, never on the fork-writable trigger variant, so no new cross-trust-boundary write path is introduced; STATE.md's existing advisory tracking of fork-trigger risk is unchanged. |
| T-01-07-02 | Tampering | manifest-derived values reaching the fetch shell | high | mitigate | Inherited and re-asserted from Plan 01-04's T-01-04-01: entries are emitted only after `corpora.Validate` accepts every one, and the fetch target re-validates with a strict allowlist before any interpolation. Two independent gates, and the second exists precisely because environment values may not have come through the first. |
| T-01-07-03 | Repudiation | vacuous CI pass | high | mitigate | Three positive assertions. | T-01-07-04 | Denial of Service | cache storage exhaustion | low | accept | **D-13's "10 GB per-repo ceiling with LRU eviction" premise is retired, not inherited.** That figure describes GitHub's cache service; this job runs on a Namespace runner where the cache is a provisioned volume. Stale directories from superseded pins accumulate and are harmless; pruning them is out of scope and deliberately not automated, since deletion logic against a shared volume is a larger blast radius than the disk it reclaims. |
