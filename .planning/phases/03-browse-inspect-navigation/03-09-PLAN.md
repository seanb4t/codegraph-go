<objective>
Make every failure mode say what happened, and ship the rebuilt bundle.

NAV-04's three states arrive by two different mechanisms, and that asymmetry is why this
is shared plumbing rather than per-view handling. `GetStatus` is the only RPC that answers
when the store is degraded; every other handler returns a Connect error carrying a typed
detail. So no-index and stale-index arrive as a successful `GetStatus` ANSWER, while
symbol-not-found arrives as a Connect ERROR from the specific call. A client that only
handled empty responses would render a blank pane in exactly the two cases this
requirement exists to prevent.

Building this per-view means writing it four times and drifting three of them: Phases 4, 5
and 6 hit the same degraded server. So the status store and the error mapper live in
`web/src/lib/`, and the banner lives in the layout every view inherits.

One refinement the phase discussion settled: the source pane's three-way collapse is
correct for two of its three causes. An absent file path and a confinement rejection are
both things the browser cannot act on. A file removed since indexing is different — it is
the stale-index case, and the user CAN act on it by re-indexing. That split needs no proto
change: the stale flag is already on the wire.

Purpose: this is criterion 5's first half, and it is the last thing between the phase's
features and a shippable bundle.

Output: the shared status gate, the layout banner, the completed degrade rendering, and a
rebuilt, re-committed `web/build/` with both drift digests green.
</objective>

`createStatusGate(client)` holding the current verdict.

both degrade cases, which is precisely why the wire carries a separate field distinguishing

developer will hit first.

repeating timer and do not schedule a delayed re-fetch: Phase 6 replaces this mechanism
with live push, so this is deliberately a small seam rather than a polling subsystem
someone has to tear out. A developer who re-indexes mid-session sees the change on their
next click, which is the honest cost of not polling.
