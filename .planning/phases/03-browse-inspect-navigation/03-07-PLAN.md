<objective>
Turn the tracer's single file view into the phase's actual browsing motion: open a symbol,
see its source with its callers, callees and blast radius, click any of them, and keep
going — with every state you reach addressable and every step walkable by back and
forward.

The history rule is the design. Navigation pushes; refinement replaces. Opening a node,
clicking a neighbour and picking a search result are places a developer wants to come
back to. Typing in search and dragging a depth control are not — but the URL must still be
correct and shareable at every instant of them. That is what "walk that history correctly"
actually asks for.

The mechanism matters as much as the rule. SvelteKit exports two different things that
both touch history, and only one of them updates the reactive URL the view reads. This
plan uses `goto` with its own `replaceState` option; a plan built on the other pair would
produce history entries whose URLs never change, passing a naive "an entry was created"
check while failing NAV-01 outright.

Purpose: BRW-03's click-through is the phase goal's own wording — keep clicking outward
without losing their place — and NAV-01's parameter shape is what Phase 4's workbench
deep-links against, so it is settled here.

Output: a navigation module, a neighbours panel, and the extension of the load seam to
symbol targets and blast radius.
</objective>

**One generation for the whole navigation, not one per call.** Cycle-1 review (Codex,
MEDIUM) found that this plan discussed aborting individual loads but never defined a
single identity spanning them, so `loadBrowseTarget` and `loadBlastRadius` could each
correctly supersede their own predecessor while the view still combined results from two
different URL states. Export a `NavigationGeneration` — a monotonically increasing counter
minted ONCE per URL change and stamped onto every load started for that URL — and have
each resolving handler compare its stamp against the current generation before committing
anything. A stale stamp is discarded whether or not its abort signal fired, because abort
is best-effort and a response already in flight can still arrive. The abort handles the
common case; the generation handles the race the abort loses. This is the same
belt-and-braces pairing plan 03-06's search controller uses, and for the same reason.

The generation is minted by the route module from the URL, not by the loaders — two
loaders minting their own generations is the defect, not the fix.
